Design, Build, Ship · Assignment 1 · Accelerated Prototyping

25 landing pages for one portfolio

A process gallery for Anees Amjad’s professional portfolio. Each version tests a different direction; later versions converge on the one that best serves the people reading it.

Audience
AI engineering recruiters, startup teams, potential collaborators
Visitors should understand
What Anees builds, what Anees personally contributed, and how to get in touch
Positioning
“I build AI tools and data products for real-world decisions.”

Final choice

Not chosen yet. Exploration has just started.

Versions

  1. v01Project-first editorial

    HypothesisRecruiters skim for evidence of real work, so leading with one project (PublicPath) and spelling out my exact contribution in a separate box will communicate more than a list of skills. An asymmetric, magazine-style layout should make that lead project feel deliberate rather than templated.

    Revision 1First screenshots showed the headline filling the whole first screen, so the "project-first" page didn't actually show the project there. Shrank the hero, removed a paragraph that repeated the contribution box, and dropped the staggered indents in the secondary list (they read as misalignment).

    EvaluationPending review.

  2. v02Terminal session

    HypothesisEngineers and technical recruiters read a command line as home turf. Framing the portfolio as a terminal session (whoami, ls projects/, cat README.md) should signal engineering fluency before a word is read, and the README gives PublicPath a natural lead slot. Risk: may read as a gimmick to non-technical visitors.

    EvaluationPending review.

  3. v03Policy memo

    HypothesisShift tone toward the public-sector half of my work. A government memorandum (TO/FROM/RE, bottom line up front, numbered record of work) matches how policy teams read, and makes the MSCAPP identity visible. Risk: may feel dry or formal to startup readers.

    EvaluationPending review.

  4. v04Pipeline schematic

    HypothesisShow my contribution instead of describing it. Each project is drawn as a pipeline on engineering grid paper, and the stages I built are highlighted in orange, so a recruiter sees my exact part of each system at a glance. Risk: the pipeline metaphor fits PublicPath well but is a stretch for projects without clear stages.

    EvaluationPending review.

  5. v05Brutalist manifesto

    HypothesisTest loudness as a strategy: enormous stacked type, hard black borders, one signal colour. Does an opinionated, poster-like page make me memorable among many portfolios, or does it overwhelm the content? The headline restates the positioning line as a manifesto.

    EvaluationPending review.

  6. v06Chicago work map

    HypothesisUse place as the organizing idea. A stylised Chicago street grid with Lake Michigan, where each project is a pin and PublicPath is the big red one; a legend panel carries the detail. Ties the page to Chicago and to the geospatial side of my work. Risk: the metaphor is decorative, since pins are projects, not addresses (the page says so).

    EvaluationPending review.

  7. v07One calm column

    HypothesisStrip everything back: one narrow column of plain sentences, no cards, no colour blocks, no visual tricks. Tests whether clear writing alone is enough for a recruiter, and whether quietness reads as confidence or as too little effort.

    EvaluationPending review.

  8. v08Recruiter scorecard

    HypothesisDesign for the skim. A candidate-snapshot card and a project table (contribution, stack, area) with working filter buttons in plain JavaScript. Where my brief has no stack for a project, the table says “Not listed” rather than guessing, which also exposes the content gap. Risk: feels like a database, not a person.

    EvaluationPending review.

  9. v09Newspaper front page

    HypothesisTreat PublicPath as front-page news: masthead, headline, deck, drop cap, an “At a glance” box, and the other projects as briefs in a side rail. Tests whether a story framing makes the lead project feel more significant than a list item does.

    EvaluationPending review.

  10. v10Bento grid

    HypothesisA modern product-page grid: tiles of different sizes and soft colours, with PublicPath, my part, the stack, and contact each getting their own tile. Tests whether chunking the content lets a visitor take in everything at a glance, versus feeling generic and template-like.

    EvaluationPending review.

  11. v11Choose your path

    HypothesisLet the visitor say who they are (hiring, building a startup, collaborating) and reshape the page: same facts, different order, framing, and call to action, plus an accent colour per path. Vanilla JS; without JS all three views show in sequence. Risk: an extra click before any content.

    EvaluationPending review.

Plain HTML and CSS. No frameworks, no build step, no external APIs, no data storage.
