Design, Build, Ship · Assignment 1 · Accelerated Prototyping

25 landing pages for one portfolio

A process gallery for Anees Amjad’s professional portfolio. Each version tests a different direction; later versions converge on the one that best serves the people reading it.

Audience
AI engineering recruiters, startup teams, potential collaborators
Visitors should understand
What Anees builds, what Anees personally contributed, and how to get in touch
Positioning
“I build AI tools and data products for real-world decisions.”

Final choice

Not chosen yet. Anees is comparing v13–v19; v20–v25 will converge on the chosen direction.

Process so far

  1. v01–v12 · Go wide

    Twelve deliberately different directions: editorial, terminal, policy memo, pipeline schematic, brutalist, map, single column, scorecard, newspaper, bento, audience selector, exhibition.

  2. Review input

    An external AI-assisted design review (not user testing) evaluated v01–v12 and recommended combining v01’s editorial type and project-first order, v04’s explicit contribution and system diagrams, and v12’s spacing and concise labels. Its notes are shown on each card, attributed, until Anees records an evaluation.

  3. Content upgrade

    From v13, content comes from Anees’s CV and checked project evidence: real links, a live PublicPath screenshot, a team dashboard image, and diagrams labelled conceptual where the work is private.

  4. v13–v19 · Recombine

    Seven structurally different compositions built on the same principles, so the choice is between real alternatives, not variations of one template. v20–v25 come after Anees reviews these.

  5. Distinctness audit (v01–v20)

    Closest relatives, flagged honestly: v01→v13 (same serif rail; v13 adds case studies), v04→v14 (both pipeline diagrams; v14 makes them primary), v08→v18 (both tables; rows are projects vs. capabilities), v10→v20 (both grids; v20 keeps one whole frame per project), v12→v15 (both exhibitions; v15 swaps art for real artifacts). The rest differ in composition and interaction. v21 and v22 are intentionally near-identical, as a controlled comparison. This audit covers only this gallery, not classmates’ work.

  6. v20–v22 · Converge

    v20 closes the broad exploration. v21 (Ourea Brain first) and v22 (PublicPath first) share one visual system so the only variable is which project introduces the portfolio. Anees has not yet chosen between them.

Factual corrections log

Content fixes, kept separate from design decisions. Layouts were not changed.

Versions

  1. v01Project-first editorial

    HypothesisRecruiters skim for evidence of real work, so leading with one project (PublicPath) and spelling out my exact contribution in a separate box will communicate more than a list of skills. An asymmetric, magazine-style layout should make that lead project feel deliberate rather than templated.

    Revision 1First screenshots showed the headline filling the whole first screen, so the "project-first" page didn't actually show the project there. Shrank the hero, removed a paragraph that repeated the contribution box, and dropped the staggered indents in the secondary list (they read as misalignment).

    External review inputStrong professional foundation; retain typography and ownership clarity, but deepen secondary-project evidence.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  2. v02Terminal session

    HypothesisEngineers and technical recruiters read a command line as home turf. Framing the portfolio as a terminal session (whoami, ls projects/, cat README.md) should signal engineering fluency before a word is read, and the README gives PublicPath a natural lead slot. Risk: may read as a gimmick to non-technical visitors.

    External review inputCoherent terminal theme, but simulated commands add overhead without proving engineering ability.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  3. v03Policy memo

    HypothesisShift tone toward the public-sector half of my work. A government memorandum (TO/FROM/RE, bottom line up front, numbered record of work) matches how policy teams read, and makes the MSCAPP identity visible. Risk: may feel dry or formal to startup readers.

    External review inputUseful structure and policy connection, but the memorandum identity overshadows the person and feels overly formal.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  4. v04Pipeline schematic

    HypothesisShow my contribution instead of describing it. Each project is drawn as a pipeline on engineering grid paper, and the stages I built are highlighted in orange, so a recruiter sees my exact part of each system at a glance. Risk: the pipeline metaphor fits PublicPath well but is a stretch for projects without clear stages.

    External review inputBest ownership explanation; retain highlighted stages, but remove diagrams that merely repeat prose.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  5. v05Brutalist manifesto

    HypothesisTest loudness as a strategy: enormous stacked type, hard black borders, one signal colour. Does an opinionated, poster-like page make me memorable among many portfolios, or does it overwhelm the content? The headline restates the positioning line as a manifesto.

    External review inputMemorable scale, but the slogan overwhelms the evidence.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  6. v06Chicago work map

    HypothesisUse place as the organizing idea. A stylised Chicago street grid with Lake Michigan, where each project is a pin and PublicPath is the big red one; a legend panel carries the detail. Ties the page to Chicago and to the geospatial side of my work. Risk: the metaphor is decorative, since pins are projects, not addresses (the page says so).

    External review inputPersonal Chicago reference, but non-geographic pins make the map mostly decorative.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  7. v07One calm column

    HypothesisStrip everything back: one narrow column of plain sentences, no cards, no colour blocks, no visual tricks. Tests whether clear writing alone is enough for a recruiter, and whether quietness reads as confidence or as too little effort.

    External review inputReadable and direct, but too little hierarchy for substantial work.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  8. v08Recruiter scorecard

    HypothesisDesign for the skim. A candidate-snapshot card and a project table (contribution, stack, area) with working filter buttons in plain JavaScript. Where my brief has no stack for a project, the table says “Not listed” rather than guessing, which also exposes the content gap. Risk: feels like a database, not a person.

    External review inputUseful comparison structure, but empty fields and database styling feel unfinished and impersonal.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  9. v09Newspaper front page

    HypothesisTreat PublicPath as front-page news: masthead, headline, deck, drop cap, an “At a glance” box, and the other projects as briefs in a side rail. Tests whether a story framing makes the lead project feel more significant than a list item does.

    External review inputStrong lead-story hierarchy, but repetition and third-person voice weaken the personal introduction.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  10. v10Bento grid

    HypothesisA modern product-page grid: tiles of different sizes and soft colours, with PublicPath, my part, the stack, and contact each getting their own tile. Tests whether chunking the content lets a visitor take in everything at a glance, versus feeling generic and template-like.

    External review inputEfficient scanning, but separate tiles fragment each project’s story.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  11. v11Choose your path

    HypothesisLet the visitor say who they are (hiring, building a startup, collaborating) and reshape the page: same facts, different order, framing, and call to action, plus an accent colour per path. Vanilla JS; without JS all three views show in sequence. Risk: an extra click before any content.

    External review inputFunctional audience adaptation, but the selector delays evidence and duplicates similar information.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  12. v12Museum exhibition

    HypothesisWildcard: present the work as an exhibition, with entrance wall text, framed abstract illustrations, and museum labels (title, medium = stack, role). Tests whether an unexpected, crafted framing makes the portfolio memorable without hiding the facts. Illustrations are labelled as not screenshots.

    External review inputThoughtful presentation, but abstract artwork and artist framing distract from engineering evidence.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  13. v13Editorial casebook

    HypothesisEach project as a short case study: problem, my contribution, a compact system figure with my stages highlighted, then evidence. A compact hero plus a contents list keeps PublicPath on the first screen at laptop size.

    Builds on / rejectsKeeps v01’s serif type, asymmetric rail and project-first order; v04’s highlighted ‘my work’ stages, now small and only where they add information; v12’s concise labels (Role, Context, Stack). Rejects v05’s oversized slogan and v12’s abstract art; uses a real PublicPath screenshot instead.

    External review inputBest overall reading structure; retain case-study rhythm and role clarity, but shorten the opening and bring evidence higher.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  14. v14System atlas

    HypothesisLet diagrams carry the structure: each project is a numbered ‘plate’, and the prose becomes annotations keyed to the diagram, so text and figure don’t repeat each other. A legend separates what I built, what I designed, and the surrounding system.

    Builds on / rejectsBuilds on v04’s ownership highlighting but answers the review note about diagrams that merely repeat prose: here the diagram is primary and the notes explain each step once. Keeps v01’s compact, project-first opening. Rejects v04’s decorative grid paper and engineering title block.

    External review inputStrongest system explanations; retain accurate diagrams and numbered annotations, but simplify the visual scaffolding and mobile layout.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  15. v15Evidence exhibition

    HypothesisLead with real artifacts, not descriptions: the live PublicPath homepage sits beside the intro on the first screen, and the team dashboard anchors the supporting work. Private work gets plain ‘design sheet’ and ‘pipeline sheet’ cards that say what can’t be shown and why.

    Builds on / rejectsInherits v12’s deliberate spacing and short labels (Artifact, Role, I built, Tools) but replaces its abstract artwork and artist framing with actual screenshots and honest spec sheets. Rejects decorative stand-ins for evidence.

    External review inputStrong artifact presentation; retain real evidence, but prevent a large product-marketing screenshot from overshadowing my contribution.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  16. v16Index and reading pane

    HypothesisTreat the portfolio like a reading app: a persistent project index beside a substantial reading pane, with PublicPath open by default so real work is visible immediately. Optional technical detail sits in a native details element. On narrow screens (or without JavaScript) the index becomes jump links and every pane stacks.

    Builds on / rejectsKeeps v11’s idea of letting the visitor steer, but drops its mandatory choice: the work is already showing. Keeps v13’s per-project structure (role, tools, copy, flow, evidence) inside each pane. Rejects v10’s fragmented tiles; each project stays one continuous story.

    External review inputUseful browsing pattern, but hidden panels reduce immediate exposure to the full portfolio.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  17. v17Problem-led narrative

    HypothesisOrganize the page around the questions the work addresses, not project names, so a collaborator sees the problem before the tool. Each question gets a colour band with ‘What I built’ beside ‘How it works’, and my steps are marked inline. The questions come from the project descriptions and READMEs.

    Builds on / rejectsInherits v09’s strong lead-story hierarchy without its third-person voice or repetition; keeps v04’s explicit ‘my work’ marking as small inline tags. Rejects v11’s audience selector: the framing serves every visitor without a click.

    External review inputUseful problem framing, but long questions repeat content and delay project names.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  18. v18Capability matrix

    HypothesisAnswer a recruiter’s real question, ‘can this person do X?’, directly: engineering capabilities as rows, projects as columns, and every filled cell names its evidence. Full project notes sit under the matrix. On phones each capability becomes a card that lists only its evidence, with no blank cells.

    Builds on / rejectsKeeps v08’s comparison structure but drops what the review flagged: no ‘Not listed’ fields, and warm editorial type instead of database styling. Keeps v13’s featured-project copy and the real PublicPath screenshot below the matrix.

    External review inputUseful content audit, but sparse cells and decoding effort weaken the homepage.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  19. v19Annotated build sequence

    HypothesisGo deep on one build instead of wide on many: PublicPath’s pipeline as a vertical, step-by-step sequence, with what each step produces and whether it was my work or the platform’s. The other projects follow as contrasting builds seen through the same lens.

    Builds on / rejectsTakes v04’s ‘stage I built’ idea to its most explicit form, step by step. Keeps v13’s sticky role/tools summary and real screenshot beside the steps. Rejects v02’s simulated terminal: shows the engineering sequence plainly instead of dressing it as commands.

    External review inputStrong technical-detail material, but too much emphasis on one project and overly absolute output claims.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  20. v20Contact sheet

    HypothesisLast broad exploration: every project appears at once as one uniform frame, like a photographer’s contact sheet, each a complete entry (artifact or diagram, title, contribution, link to details). Featured projects get an editor’s red frame, and each frame says whether it is a real screenshot or a simplified diagram.

    Builds on / rejectsAnswers v10’s fragmentation: one frame per project instead of separate tiles for role, stack and copy. Keeps v15’s rule that real artifacts are labelled as such. Diagrams are HTML rather than SVG, so they reflow on phones instead of scrolling sideways as v14’s did.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  21. v21Synthesis, AI-first

    HypothesisFirst convergence: an editorial portfolio with visible engineering evidence, led by Ourea Brain. A compact hero with a prominent name puts the first case’s architecture on the first screen; each case gives audience, my contribution, a numbered ‘how it works’, and labelled evidence, without repeating the same paragraph.

    Builds on / rejectsStructure from v13 (rail, case rhythm, role clarity) with a shorter opening; numbered steps from v14, drawn in HTML so they read on phones; artifact treatment from v15, with the PublicPath screenshot kept small and captioned as the team’s product. Uses v19’s detail only inside ‘How it works’, rewritten as mechanisms rather than guarantees.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  22. v22Synthesis, product-first

    HypothesisControlled comparison with v21: identical visual system and case content, but PublicPath leads, the intro names the platform first, and the first-screen artifact is PublicPath’s data flow instead of Ourea Brain’s architecture. Tests whether product-building or AI-system experience is the stronger introduction.

    Builds on / rejectsDeliberately near-identical to v21; only order, numbering, intro clause order and the hero artifact change. It is a convergence step, not one of the broad explorations.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  23. v23Fast overview

    HypothesisCan a visitor understand my focus and pick a project in seconds? All three featured projects sit side by side right under a compact hero, each answering ‘what it does’, ‘what I built’ and ‘observed/artifact/output’. Full cases follow as one readable column, with the technical ‘how it works’ tucked into native disclosure elements.

    Builds on / rejectsStarts from v21 (Anees’s chosen direction), keeping its order, type and contribution marking. Removes v21’s hero diagram so the architecture appears only once, inside its case. New structure: overview band, then single-column long-read cases (not v20’s artifact frames or v16’s hidden panes).

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  24. v24Artifact-led casebook

    HypothesisLead every case with its visual: a full-width dark figure spread holding an explanatory diagram (and, for PublicPath, the captioned homepage beside the data flow), then three short columns: what it does, what I built, and observed behaviour, artifact or output. A ‘list of figures’ in the hero makes every visual findable.

    Builds on / rejectsBuilds on v21’s order and contribution marking, v14’s diagram clarity and v15’s artifact labelling, but changes the composition: figure first, text after, in full-width spreads (not v14’s side-by-side plates). Every figure states whether it is an explanatory diagram or a project artifact.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.

  25. v25Recommended synthesis

    HypothesisCombine v23’s fast choice with v24’s visible contribution diagrams: a persistent profile sidebar keeps name, intro, actions, a one-line index of all three projects and contact visible while the cases scroll; each case shows a light, labelled diagram with my stages marked, then ‘what it does / what I built / observed behaviour, artifact or output’.

    Builds on / rejectsFrom v23: all three projects discoverable at once, and tools tucked into disclosure. From v24: diagram before prose, explicit diagram/artifact labels, and a small captioned screenshot. Drops v24’s heavy dark panels and v23’s hidden diagrams. Differs from v16: nothing is hidden; the sidebar is identity plus navigation, not a pane switcher. On phones the sidebar becomes the header, then the cases.

    Anees’s evaluationPending.
